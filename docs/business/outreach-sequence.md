# Outreach sequence and channels

Status: draft · Last updated: 2026-10-27 · Source: owner sessions 2026-10-26/27

How the three outbound channels work together for one company, and who to contact inside it. Calls are in
`outreach-calls.md`, email in `outreach-email.md`.

## Manual only

**Every outbound touch is done by hand, by the owner.** No automated sending, no AI-written outreach, no LinkedIn
automation tools (LinkedIn bans them). Each deal is make-or-break. Research and prep can use Kardata and AI; the owner
decides what to say and writes every word.

## Channels

| Channel | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Warms up the contact, and credibility through posts | Strongest where owners are active (D2C) |
| Cold calls | Discovery and the fastest learning | Strongest where owners rarely use LinkedIn (trades, clinics, bakeries) |
| Cold email | Scale, and buyers without phone numbers | Goal is a reply, not a meeting. The reply is where discovery starts |

**Rank the channels per sector, not overall.** After 4–6 weeks, judge them by meetings booked per channel (`funnel.md`).

## The sequence for one contact

1. **Profile ready first.** A plain headline, a 3–4 line About section, and ideally 1–2 posts. An empty or vague
   profile hurts more than not connecting.
2. **The week before (optional): LinkedIn connection,** no note or one neutral line, no pitch. It costs no momentum.
3. **One compressed week, every channel (D-63).** Interest fades over weeks, so the whole push and the booking happen
   inside one week:
   - **Day 1:** email (`outreach-email.md`). Only public facts. Never use anything from a practice call.
   - **Day 2–3:** call, with the after-email opener (`outreach-calls.md`). Book on the call.
   - **Day 4–5:** a second call attempt, or a short LinkedIn message if they accepted.
   - **The meeting lands the same week or the next.** Send the calendar invite during the call.
4. **One last note about a week later,** with one useful insight and no ask. Then stop.

**Timing:** skip the buyer's peak season. Run the week when they have time to talk now, not to book far ahead.

## Who to contact inside a company

- **Top-down by default.** In a founder-led company, the founder usually approves the spend. A forward from the
  founder ("X suggested I get in touch") beats any cold approach to their team.
- **No reply after about 10 days:** go to the most senior operations person, with an angle built on their role.
- **Multi-threading:** at most **2 people at once** in a small company, each with a different angle, staggered 3–5
  days apart. Be open about it if it comes up. **When one replies, pause the other.** Never contact the founder and
  their director in the same week.
- **Junior or hands-on people** (warehouse, accounts): the best source of truth, not the buyer. Reach them
  **through** the decision-maker, for example in the audit. Long tenure makes them more valuable, not a buyer.
- Check titles against the team page or LinkedIn. Apollo titles and staff counts are often out of date.
- **Emails and phone numbers come only from Apollo** (D-67). Names and roles may come from public sources. Never
  call a company's main line.
