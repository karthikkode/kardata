# Problem bar — when a problem is worth pitching

Status: locked (D2C signals are a draft) · Last updated: 2026-10-26 · Source: kardata_old/campaign/problem-bar.md
v3 + problems-brief.md contract v3

## The five tests (all must pass)

1. **It costs something we can count.** Volume × time × money, even as a rough estimate.
   - "Bad reviews" fails.
   - "The same 3 complaints across 25+ dated reviews in 6 months" passes.
2. **It's a system gap, not a staffing gap.** The fix needs visibility they don't have, not people they haven't
   hired. If "hire 2 people" solves it, we have no opening and we walk away.
3. **It repeats.** It happens weekly, so ongoing reporting and fixes make sense. One-off messes are projects, not
   retainers.
4. **The budget owner feels it.** The founder or COO feels it as lost sales, refunds, stock-outs or wasted payroll.
   It isn't just a nicer dashboard for an analyst.
5. **One question confirms it.** Public signal plus one call question confirms the problem. If we can't name that
   question, we don't understand the problem well enough to pitch it.

## The eight dimensions (check every company on all eight)

Mark each one **evidence found** (with sources and dates) or **none found**. "None found" is a result, and skipping
a dimension silently fails the check.

| # | Dimension | D2C signals |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Labour:** repeatable hand work | ops/analyst/Excel job openings, JD quotes about manual reports, data entered twice |
| 2 | **Time to value:** how fast things go live | slow launches to new retailers, long gaps between a launch and it appearing in stores |
| 3 | **Revenue capture:** money not collected | products bought again with no subscription, best sellers out of stock, abandoned retail channels |
| 4 | **Retention:** customers who stop buying | dated review themes about order or quality problems, a gap in repeat purchase, loyalty programs nobody uses |
| 5 | **Compliance / risk:** deadlines, penalties | retailer chargebacks and compliance rules, product claims rules, recalls |
| 6 | **Accuracy / rework:** errors, re-doing work | wrong orders in reviews, mismatched stock, conflicting numbers across channels |
| 7 | **Visibility:** what the founder can't see | many channels with no tool joining them, spreadsheet-based reporting, "we just check the bank" |
| 8 | **Growth limit:** one person or team holding everything | one ops hire owns the whole function, the founder does production planning by hand |

A verdict can lead with any dimension. **Don't default to "hours saved".** The stake follows the dimension the
evidence actually hit.

## Estimating the value (internal only)

Use the value formula in `offer.md`. Always say which part of the estimate is priced:

- **Part A, hours:** repeatable hours × hourly cost, assuming 160 working hours a month.
- **Part B, revenue at risk:** an observable signal × volume × value, labelled as an estimate with the inputs
  shown.
  - **If there's no signal we can observe, don't price Part B.** Price Part A only and say so. Invented revenue is
    how bias gets back in.
- **Part C, risk exposure:** deadlines and penalties, described in words and never priced.

**Size bands:**

| Recoverable value per month | Shape | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **$9k or more** | Retainer-sized (3× Core) | Pitch normally |
| **$4–9k** | Audit-sized | Still send; inside data often shows more. Mark it `audit-shaped` in the tracker |
| **Under $4k** | Lowest priority | Send last, and never research new companies of this size |

## Rules against bias

1. **Hiring signals can support a verdict but never be its only evidence.** Every verdict needs 2+ independent
   signals, and at least one must not be hiring.
2. **Signals that rule a company out apply on every dimension:**
   - customers praising the support team;
   - help pages that work well;
   - an existing data team (a tech team without data roles does not count, see `icp.md`);
   - failing the ICP.

   **A new dimension never brings an excluded company back.**
3. **The outside view is a sample, not a diagnosis.** The pitch asks about the pattern, and the audit answers it.
