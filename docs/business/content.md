# Content

Status: draft (reworked in the content session; open points D-46 and D-49) · Last updated: 2026-10-26 · Source:
owner content session 2026-10-26 (D-39 to D-53), kardata_old/campaign/case-study-framework.md (the rules kept below)

Content supports outbound and never replaces it. Calls are still the core of the 100-day goal. Content gets
**about 4–6 hours a week** (D-44).

**What content sells: the audit, never the fix (D-53, D-54).** Every touch with a new prospect, outbound or inbound,
sells either the audit or a short call to sell it. Builds and retainers come later. The audit finds where
time and money are being wasted, so that is what every piece is about.

## The four jobs of every piece (D-53)

The reader should come away sure that this person:
1. **knows what he's talking about** (sourced, specific, no filler);
2. **has breadth** (the story comes from a field they'd never think of);
3. **can find a problem and solve it** (the structured steps);
4. **thinks in their money and time** (the estimate is in a D2C brand's own units).

## Who it is for, and how we know it works (D-41)

- **Audience: D2C brand owners only, in any category (D-52).** Skincare is only the first research sector; examples
  should work for any D2C brand. Owners who can pay $1–6k: about $1M–20M revenue, selling through more than one
  channel or system, owner still close to operations. Not learners, not other consultants, not other sectors.
- **What we count:**
  - owners who reply to a post or send a DM;
  - pieces sent to a prospect (before or after a call);
  - "I saw your post" said in a meeting.
- **What we ignore:** views, likes, follower counts. A post that 40 people saw and one owner answered beat a post
  that 4,000 people saw.

## Theme (D-39)

**The everyday decisions a business makes under uncertainty, what's at stake, and the numbers behind them.** Time
and money are the scoreboard; decisions are the game.
- **Framing decides the audience.** The hook is always the owner's problem and its stakes, in their words ("Three
  reports. Three different numbers for last week."). Never the concept.
- **The concept comes inside, named once,** as the "how": operations research, queueing, game theory, inventory
  theory and so on.
- **The bridge to the audit:** every good rule needs a number the business already has but nobody has worked out
  (a sell-through rate, a repeat rate, a stock-out count). Finding those numbers is the audit.
- **The problem is always a D2C problem (D-52).** Only the story in step 2 may come from another field.
- **Content sells the audit indirectly and never the fix.**

## The shape of every blog piece (D-42, refined by D-50)

1. **Establish the D2C problem:** the decision as the owner meets it, and an honest estimate of what it costs (inputs
   shown; a guess is labelled as one, with a range).
2. **The story:** a real case from any field where a similar problem was solved in a way that isn't obvious. Sourced
   (paper, award entry, primary record). The non-obvious part is the point; a case everyone already knows teaches
   nothing.
3. **The mapping:** how that solution carries over to the D2C problem, then structured steps the owner can follow to
   find the problem in their own business and start fixing it. Concept named once. Then a short **Limits** paragraph:
   where the analogy breaks.
4. **The offer:** something the reader can use straight away (format open, D-51), then the quiet line: "This is what
   I look for in an audit." No call to action.

The LinkedIn posts are short cuts from the blog: one problem, one story beat, or one step each.

## Mix (D-42)

| Share | Kind | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| ~50% | Audits, on camera or in writing | Synthetic businesses (always labelled as made up), or real ones with written permission. Never call out a named company (D-09) |
| ~30% | One leak explained | One decision, one cost, one check |
| ~20% | Concepts and case studies | Still opens on an owner's problem |

Until the first client or permission exists, the audit share is synthetic walkthroughs.

## Channels (D-40, D-43)

| Order | Channel | Cadence | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Website blog | One piece a week (D-47) | Does the real work: what we send after a call, what people find when they search us |
| 1 | LinkedIn posts | 2–3 a week, cut from that week's blog | Warms up outbound: short versions, like a reel of the blog |
| 2 | YouTube | Weekly 15–25 min main video plus clips; optional raw hour-long live brainstorm with AI on camera | The long-term inbound engine. Start date open (D-49) |
| 3 | Instagram | Clips only | Last |

- **The blog version is written first.** The post is cut from it, never the other way round.
- **No "part 1 of 5" chains, no "read the blog" teasers.** Each post stands on its own.

## Writing rules (D-44, D-45)

- **Written by the owner, by hand.** No AI-written posts. AI may help find and check sources, never draft the words.
- **No engagement tactics:** no "comment X for a DM", no hooks that promise a secret, no AI-slop images.
- **Confident, not clever.** Every piece leaves the owner one thing to check.
- **Every claim is real and sourced.** No invented numbers. Check every figure against the original source before
  posting, not against a blog that quotes it.
- **The generic test:** could this sentence appear in any AI-written post? Make it specific or cut it.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** "3.5% more revenue on a weekday" beats "significantly more".
- **Say what kind of number it is:** a study result, a company's own claim, a vendor's data, or my estimate.
- **Name companies only for public, checkable, neutral facts** (an award entry, a published paper).

## Sector benchmark pieces (kept from the old framework)

When we publish patterns across a sector from our own research, the old structure still applies: method (sample
size and how it was chosen), patterns with frequencies over a stated sample ("31 of 152"), one thesis with the other
side steelmanned, and a mandatory **Limits** section. Aggregates only; no company is identifiable (D-09).

## Checks before publishing

- [ ] The hook is the owner's problem, not the concept.
- [ ] Every number is checked against its original source, and its kind is stated (study, company claim, vendor,
      estimate).
- [ ] Outside statistics meet the source rule (two independent sources, or see D-46).
- [ ] No real company is named in a negative light; synthetic businesses are labelled as synthetic.
- [ ] No internal value estimates about a real prospect.
- [ ] It ends with one thing to check and the quiet audit line.
- [ ] Opportunity, never fear (D-03).
- [ ] Written by hand, start to finish.
