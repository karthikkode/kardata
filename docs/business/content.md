Content supports outreach and never replaces it. **90% outreach, 10% content** (about 3–4 hours a week): one case
study every 2–3 weeks, plus 1–2 LinkedIn posts a week taken from it.
- Before writing any piece, name its job: something to find when people search you, something to leave after a
  call, something to use on a call, or something that gives you hooks.
- The audience is business owners, not learners.
- **Proof of work only.** No AI-sounding writing, and no "comment X for a DM" tactics.

## Case study structure (each section answers the question the last one raised)

1. **Hook:** context before the surprise.
   - First say what's possible now, the problem in the sector, and the fix.
   - Then say what companies actually do instead.
   - Then one observed fact, specific enough that no generic post could contain it.
2. **Stakes:** the cost, with real numbers.
3. **Method:** sample size, sources, what was inspected and what was inferred.
4. **Patterns:** each with its frequency over a stated sample ("31 of 152"), examples, why it happens, and what it
   costs.
5. **Thesis:** one original argument. Steelman the other side, then answer it. **No thesis, no publish.**
6. **What opens up:** per pattern, what changes, for whom, in their units.
7. **Limits:** selection bias, what's unverified, open questions. This section is mandatory.
8. **Bridge:** "This is the same analysis I run on a company's own data." Then one genuine question to readers.

## Rules that keep it from sounding like AI

- Every claim has a sample size or a source. Never write "many companies" or "studies show".
- **The generic test:** could this sentence appear in any AI-written post? If so, make it specific or cut it.
- Numbers over adjectives.
- Name companies only for public, checkable, neutral facts.
- Always show the work. Method and Limits are required sections.

## Two channels

| | Website case study | LinkedIn |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Founders arriving from outreach | Broad feed: founders, peers |
| Job | Prove the sector problem exists and that Karthik fills the gap | Karthik's own view: why the data comes before AI |
| Shape | One detailed piece | Several self-contained posts, each with one finding, one number, why it happens, and the bridge |

The website goes first, always. Never use "part 1 of 5" chains or "read the blog" teasers.

## Checks before publishing

- [ ] Every frequency states its sample and source. There's a one-line note on how the sample was chosen.
- [ ] Every outside statistic is confirmed by two independent sources.
- [ ] No company is named in a negative pattern without public evidence anyone can check.
- [ ] No internal value estimates.
- [ ] Opportunity, never fear.
- [ ] Ends with the bridge sentence.
