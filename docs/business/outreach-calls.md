# Cold calls

Status: locked (practice in progress) · Last updated: 2026-10-26 · Source: owner coaching sessions 2026-10-23/24/26

**Why we call:** fast feedback, hearing the real concerns, and the owner's strength at asking questions. **The call
sells the meeting, not the service.**

## Before the call (about 3 minutes per company, about 1 hour per sector)

**Sector prep kit (once per sector):**
- How they make money, and the words they use for it.
- **What the owner cares about most.** Lead with it and the money follows. A clinic owner cares about patient
  outcomes; a trades owner about the family name and their techs.
- What they measure, and where money usually leaks. Keep 3 concrete examples ready for "be specific".
- Their hard daily decisions (how much to make, who goes first, which channel gets the stock).
- Their seasons, their systems, and what they're proud of.
- Rule: never put a word in the opener that you can't explain in one sentence.

**Company brief (from Kardata, then the press):**
- Read the Kardata dossier, **then check trade press and news.** A research run can miss big events such as
  acquisitions.
- Sort the signals into **solution in motion** (a job post, a new tool, an agency, a new building) and
  **unaddressed** (complaints, contradictions, gaps).
- **Open on an unaddressed signal.** Never challenge a solution they chose.
- **Check every signal before using it.** For example, "unavailable on their site, in stock at a retailer" can just be
  two versions of the same product.
- Treat vendor numbers (Apollo staff count, revenue) as unverified. Never say them out loud.
- Know who usually picks up, what time it is there, and when the buyer is busy (production, packing hours, peak
  season).

**Scenario map (the real prep):** for the first question, list the likely answers and the next move for each. A
specific question can't trap you if you've mapped where each answer leads.

| They say | Next move |
|---|---|
| "Pretty quickly" / "we've got it" | Ask how: "From their weekly reports?" |
| "End of the month" | One implication question (below) |
| "X handles it" | Ask how X does it, the last time, or what's coming |
| "It's fine" | A question about what's coming next (a new brand, a new retailer) |
| "Why do you ask?" / "Who are you?" | Exit signal: answer, then close |
| "Not interested" / "We're slammed" | Graceful exit and permission to check back |

## Call map

1. **Opener:**
   - "Hi <name>, Karthik from kardata. This is a cold call. Have you got 30 seconds?"
   - After an email: "I emailed you Tuesday. You probably haven't seen it, so this is basically a cold call. Have
     you got 30 seconds?" **Never ask "did you see my email?"**
   - Then the one-liner in their language, why them, and **one** question about a recent action of theirs.
   - **No** "How's your day?". **No** yes/no "Is this relevant?".
2. **Discovery:** 1–3 questions, **one at a time,** funnel-shaped (below).
3. **Play their pain back** in their own words.
4. **Close.**

## Discovery that doesn't offend

- **The funnel:** start with a broad frame, ask one specific question, and build each next question from their last
  words. The close is the narrowest point. **Don't reuse a prepared question once they've given you a fresh clue.**
  - Frame + question: "You've grown fast: new brands, more retailers. When a retailer's sales start slipping, how soon
    do you usually find out?"
- **Ask about a recent action, never about their process in general.** "How do you make decisions?" or "are your
  decisions data-backed?" sounds like an accusation. "When you added that retailer, how did you see a few months later
  whether it was worth it?" is neutral.
- **Ask how things work,** the last time it happened, and who does what.
- **Hand-off question:** "When [specific event], how does it get from [A] to [B]?"
  - Example: "When a retailer sends a PO, how does it get into your store system?"
- **Clue words to listen for:** "usually", "roughly", "we just", "someone handles it", "it's fine", "monthly".
- **"X handles it" isn't an answer to attack or flatter.** Be curious: how X does it, the last time, what's coming.
- **Never ask "is there a bottleneck?"** or any yes/no question about a problem. The answer is always no. Ask them to
  describe it, and judge for yourself.
- **Never quote a complaint or bad review back to them.** Ask about the process behind it.
- **No** generic praise, no assumptions about volume, no leading tail that gives away the problem.

## Letting them see the cost (implication questions)

Don't tell them it's slow or inefficient. Ask questions until they work the cost out themselves, in their currency.
(The method comes from Neil Rackham's *SPIN Selling*.)
- **Delay:** "How soon do you usually find out?" **Money:** "Last time, what did the late catch cost?"
- **Scale:** "With the new brand, who builds those?" **Risk:** "If X is off for a month, what happens?"
- **Value, said by them:** "If you could see it every week without anyone building it, what would you do
  differently?" Mirror the answer in the close.
- **On a call: one or two at most.** The full costing is the meeting and the paid diagnostic. Never invent the number
  for them.

## When to stop asking and close (exit signals)

- They name a pain in detail.
- They ask about you: "why?", "why do you ask?", "who are you?", "where's this going?", "what do you sell?",
  "is this a dashboard thing?"
- You've asked 3 questions.
- A time cue: "I've got to go", "we're packing", "I'm pulling in".

## Close

1. **Mirror their exact words** ("Boots sends one format, another retailer sends another…").
2. "That's exactly what I work on."
3. **What they get in 30 minutes (D-60).** Promise what they'll get, never proof you don't have ("a real case study").
4. **Two specific slots in their time zone**, said plainly ("UK time", "Mountain"). If you don't know the zone, confirm
   it while scheduling.
5. **Respect their timing.** In peak season or right after a big event, offer slots after it. Don't fight for this
   week.

Example: "Thirty minutes Tuesday 10am or Wednesday 2pm Central?"

**If it really is fine:** accept it and leave well. "Sounds like it's working. Can I check back in the new year?" Most
calls end here, and a polite exit keeps the door open.

## "What do you sell?" (in any form)

- **Answer it the first time they ask,** with the one-liner narrowed to their pain: "I help [brands like yours] turn
  [their mess] into [a clear outcome]."
- Make it different from the vendors they hear from every week. For example: "Not a dashboard. Dashboards show what
  happened. I help you see whether a decision is paying off."
- Say **"I"**, not "we" or "agency". "Agency" makes people think of a marketing agency.
- **Bridge past work from another sector in one sentence:** "Different sector, same problem: data from lots of partners
  in different formats."
- **Never dodge it** to ask more questions first, and never give a vague answer ("to understand where I can help").

## "We already have a CRM / Klaviyo / a dashboard"

- A CRM or Klaviyo unifies **the customer** (one profile per shopper). A decision needs **the business**: retailer
  reports, Amazon, stock, costs, returns, every brand. **The CRM is one of the systems we connect, not an alternative
  to one data layer.**
- Never knock their tool; it's a solution in motion. Complement it: "Makes sense. When a retailer report comes in, does
  that end up in there too, or somewhere else?"
- Brands selling only on their own site are a weaker fit; their tools cover most of it. Ask one gap question (profit
  per order after ads and returns), and exit politely if it's covered.

## Gatekeepers

- They're allies. **The win is the map:** who does what, who decides, and when to call back.
- **Listen to who actually picks up** before using a name.

## The owner's recurring gaps (watch these)

- **Openers:** generic jargon in the opener ("optimise time", "revenue leaks", "track the right metrics", which invites
  "is this a dashboard thing?"); "How's your day?"; loaded words like "growing aggressively".
- **Questions:** stacking questions; "Would that help?" questions; reusing a prepared question after a fresh clue;
  asking about their decision-making directly.
- **Claims:** absolute or invented claims ("20 hours", "70%"); promising a case study you don't have.
- **Pitching:** pitching too early after hearing something useful; criticising their hire or agency.
- **Closing:** a generic close instead of their words; offering slots in their busiest week.
- **Filler and hedging:** "I can really understand your concern"; "maybe… if possible… does that work?".
- **Listening:** greeting the wrong name when someone else picks up.
- **Prep:** dialing before the scenario map is done.
