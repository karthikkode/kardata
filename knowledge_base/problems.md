# Worthy problems

## Breadth rule (anti-fixation)

Research a company across **all** problem surfaces: repeated manual
reconciliation, contradictory customer experiences, broken handoffs,
inventory or fulfillment mismatch, policy friction, poor measurement, and
decisions made without trustworthy data. Record **every** problem that
meets the evidence bar, then rank — never stop at the first find.

A single problem type must never become the whole research. Past failure
mode: entire research built around one symptom class (e.g. ads pointing at
out-of-stock products). Out-of-stock ads are one example of inventory
mismatch, not a research program. An agent whose findings collapse to one
symptom class has not finished researching the company.

## Worthiness is an evidence standard, not a taxonomy

No list of "what counts as a problem" is given — that rigidity produced
trivia escalation. A problem is worthy when the evidence proves it is
worth paying $3–6k/mo to fix: it solves the company's biggest problem or
saves substantial time/money. The admission test is MECHANISM-or-COST
evidence (see Evidence admissibility below). Nitty-gritty basics without
commercial weight do not advance, however well evidenced.

## Evidence admissibility (hard bar)

- A quote counts as problem evidence **only if it asserts a MECHANISM or
  a COST**. Page labels, nav tokens, verdict tokens, praise-section nouns,
  and bare flags ("Sold out", "Testimonial") are never problems.
- The evidence URL must share the company's registrable domain or carry
  an explicit attribution chain. Third-party pages filed as the company's
  pain are inadmissible.
- Traceability (claim → stored document → quote) is necessary but never
  sufficient; it does not upgrade inadmissible content.
- Prefer first-party operating evidence; corroborate across independent
  sources; actively record counter-evidence and freshness. Separate
  observed fact from analyst inference. Never turn absence of evidence
  into evidence of absence.

## Mail-worthiness (headroom)

A problem earns outreach only with: (a) current attributed evidence,
(b) mechanism not consequence, (c) a Headroom sentence — invisible
cost/yr versus the $36–72k/yr fee at roughly 10x headroom — or explicit
UNQUANTIFIED and no mail, (d) a fix-first framing the wedge engagement
can move.

## The skeptic

Every proposed problem must survive one hostile question before it
advances: *"Would this company pay $3–6k/mo to fix this, and what evidence
says so?"* Trivia dies in debate, not in a checklist. If the agent cannot
answer with the company's own numbers or costs, the problem does not
advance.
